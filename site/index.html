One conversation, its own group.
Agents post progress, blockers, and results to a dedicated Feishu group. The session name stays with each update, so parallel tasks are easy to tell apart.
Reply to the session you started.
Replies are routed to the matching Claude Code, Codex, or OpenCode session. Nootify uses existing clients and preserves their conversation context.
At home in your system tray.
A native window and system-tray controls sit above an independent local service. Closing the window keeps the service available; progress and routing logs remain easy to inspect.

A small app.
A shorter feedback loop.

The Windows and macOS apps include their service and Node.js runtime. You bring your coding client and a Feishu bot.

  1. Install on your computer.

    On Windows, run the x64 installer. On macOS, drag Nootify from the DMG into Applications. The first launch installs and starts your background service.

  2. Connect Feishu and your clients.

    Configure a Feishu bot and its chat permissions, then enable the clients you use on the Agents page. Follow the setup guide for credentials, trusted senders, and client connections.

  3. Start a conversation.

    New agent conversations receive instructions to open a Feishu group and post updates. Reply in that group to continue the matching session on your computer.

A few things to know.

Does Nootify run a model for me?

No. It connects to the Claude Code, Codex, or OpenCode client you already run. It does not call a model API or replace your coding agent.

Can I reply while my computer is offline?

The local service and the target session must be available. Nootify does not revive archived or inactive sessions, and a delivered message does not automatically approve a permission request.

Where does the data go?

Progress updates and replies travel through your Feishu group. Service state, routing information, and credentials are stored locally on your computer. The public website does not connect to your local service.

What happens when I close the app?

Closing the window hides it. Quitting the desktop app leaves the independent background service running. Service controls in the system tray let you manage it separately.
